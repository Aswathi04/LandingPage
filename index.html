the most "useless" landing page

here's everything you could stuff into your landing page. no rules, just a rough map so you're not staring at a blank page at 3 AM.

origin story

the spark

the exact moment the idea happened. was it a joke that went too far? a dare? someone too sleep-deprived to think straight at 11 PM? set the scene: who said what, and what made everyone go "wait... we could actually build that."

include: 2–4 sentences of the origin story + one direct quote from the moment.
before venue day

before you showed up

what prep happened before the event, not a project plan, just a chaotic to-do list.

  • what you ordered or scavenged beforehand
  • what you argued about, and who won
  • how you split up roles (bonus points if it wasn't very organized)
include: 3–5 bullet points, written like a group chat log, not a Gantt chart.
the concept

the napkin version

your first sketch, doodle, or "concept art," before any real building happened. the messier and more ambitious-looking compared to the final build, the funnier.

include: one image (hand-drawn, whiteboard photo, rough sketch) + a one-line caption roasting your own original plan.
the actual thing

what you actually built

the real project, hardware, software, or both, described plainly and a little dramatically.

  • the problem: what it pretends to solve. treat it like a TED talk about something nobody asked to be fixed.
  • the fix: what it actually does, in plain language. no jargon.
  • how it's made: a short breakdown by component: software, hardware, physical build, power.
include: at least 2–3 images or a short demo GIF/video of the actual build.
the verdict

from the team

a closing note on how the whole thing actually felt to build. best moment, worst moment, would you do it again?

include: one honest, funny quote from the team + links to GitHub / demo video / design files.
